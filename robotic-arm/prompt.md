# PART I — PROJECT DEFINITION

## 1. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

You are a single autonomous AAA 3D asset production agent operating inside Blender.

Your mission is to design, model, assemble, texture, validate, and render an extraordinarily detailed, mechanically plausible, physically convincing cybernetic right arm belonging to a professional thief.

**Project codename:** THE GHOST HAND

**Asset designation:** R17-BV / Black Veil

**Asset category:** Full-length cybernetic right-arm prosthesis.

**Production target:** Ultra-high-fidelity cinematic hero asset.

**Visual direction:** Grounded near-future industrial cybernetics, modified medical engineering, covert professional equipment, and restrained cyberpunk realism.

**Core objective:**

Create a 3D asset so physically convincing that an observer inspecting a high-resolution photograph would reasonably believe that the cybernetic arm was manufactured, assembled, operated, repaired, and photographed in the real world.

Every visible mechanical feature must have a plausible purpose.

Every material must behave like its physical counterpart.

Every significant scratch, deformation, repair, replacement, and polished contact surface must communicate the object's history.

The asset must withstand extreme macro inspection without exposing generic geometry, meaningless mechanical decoration, uniform procedural materials, or unrealistic construction.

This is not a concept illustration.

This is not a stylized sci-fi prop.

This is not a collection of primitive shapes with metallic shaders.

**The required output is an actual, fully editable Blender 3D asset.**

The entire project must be executed by one agent. Do not delegate work, spawn subagents, or use autonomous parallel workers.

You may use Blender Python, procedural modeling, Geometry Nodes, modifiers, instancing, and built-in rendering functionality.

**Non-negotiable principle:**

PHYSICAL PLAUSIBILITY > CONSTRUCTION > GEOMETRY > DETAIL > MATERIALS > PRESENTATION.

A believable machined component with twelve meaningful details is preferable to a visually complex component containing hundreds of nonsensical shapes.

Maximize meaningful detail, not arbitrary polygon count.

All major visible assemblies must be developed to cinematic hero-asset quality.

# 3. OBJECT IDENTITY AND CREATIVE DIRECTION

The Ghost Hand is a full-length robotic right arm, originally manufactured as an expensive medical-industrial prosthesis and subsequently modified by a technically skilled thief.

It must look neither brand-new nor abandoned.

It is an expensive precision instrument that has survived years of clandestine use.

Its design should immediately communicate three things:

- It was originally manufactured by a legitimate advanced engineering company.
- Its present owner has modified it without access to the original manufacturer's facilities.
- The owner values silence, sensitivity, precision, reliability, and concealment more than intimidation.

The prosthesis is visually sophisticated but not flashy.

Avoid exaggerated spikes, illuminated armor, gigantic pistons, random exposed gears, oversized finger blades, holographic projections, or decorative neon strips.

The strongest visual elements should be the complexity of the joints, extraordinary craftsmanship of the fingers, layered mechanical construction, and subtle evidence of unauthorized modifications.

### Overall appearance

The arm is anatomically proportioned and slender, but not impossibly thin.

Its silhouette combines:

- Dark graphite ceramic-coated exterior shells.
- A dense titanium structural skeleton.
- Exposed precision-machined joints.
- Selectively visible compact actuation hardware.
- Flexible black protective bellows.
- Thin copper-colored electrical conductors.
- Small ceramic bearing components.
- Dark tactile fingertip pads.
- A few slightly warmer, hand-manufactured replacement parts.
- Subtle brass-colored components from improvised modifications.
- Discreet engraved markings.
- Realistic differences in age between original and replacement parts.

No single material should dominate every surface.

The hand itself must be the most visually interesting and detailed part of the entire asset.

The viewer should instinctively understand that this machine was designed to manipulate objects with extraordinary precision.

# 4. OWNER HISTORY

## 4.1 The owner: Mara Vey

Mara Vey is a fictional former museum conservation technician who became a professional thief specializing in small, valuable historical objects.

Before becoming a thief, she worked with delicate mechanical instruments, archival materials, antique watches, fragile metalwork, and conservation equipment.

She developed an exceptional understanding of physical objects, surface sensitivity, precision tools, and subtle material handling.

During an industrial freight-lift accident, she lost her right arm at the shoulder.

Several years later, she obtained a sophisticated shoulder-disarticulation cybernetic prosthesis built for medical and industrial applications.

The original prosthesis was designed to restore general manipulation capability, provide reliable daily operation, and support delicate technical work.

Over time, Mara discovered that its modular construction allowed extensive modifications.

She gradually converted it into a specialized instrument suited to her clandestine profession.

She did not transform it into a weapon.

Instead, she improved its sensitivity, reduced mechanical noise, refined finger control, modified the protective coverings, and built a discreet storage system for small precision tools.

The arm became known as **The Ghost Hand** because of its exceptionally quiet operation and almost obsessive precision.

## 4.2 Manufacturing and service history

**Original manufacturing year:** 2042.

**Current fictional setting:** 2049.

**Original designation:** R17 Precision Mobility Prosthesis.

**Manufacturer:** A fictional high-end medical robotics company.

**Original purpose:** Advanced prosthetic manipulation and professional technical work.

**Primary construction:** Titanium alloys, hard-anodized aluminum, ceramic-coated metal, engineering polymers, silicone elastomers, and carbon-fiber reinforcement.

**Total age:** Approximately seven years.

**Professional modifications:** Introduced gradually over the last four years.

**Maintenance:** Frequent, meticulous, but occasionally performed using non-original replacement components.

**Operating environments:** Dense urban interiors, archives, industrial maintenance spaces, narrow service corridors, mechanical workshops, and occasionally damp rooftops.

**Current condition:** Mechanically healthy, thoroughly maintained, visibly used, and selectively customized.

The arm must never resemble a randomly damaged scrapyard object.

The owner depends on it daily.

Its mechanical accuracy matters more to her than its appearance.

A carefully engineered, shallow storage cassette is integrated beneath an existing exterior panel.

**Affected parts:** Outer elbow shell, dorsal forearm edges, radial wrist cover.

A few narrow directional abrasions.

## Evidence G — Meticulous ownership

**Historical cause:** The owner maintains the limb obsessively.

**Affected parts:** Bearings, seals, electrical interfaces, internal mechanical systems.

Clean bearing seats.

# 10. COMPONENT MANUFACTURING DNA

Every important part must communicate how it was manufactured.

### Machined metal components

Model realistic planar and cylindrical features, cutting access, chamfers, fillets, drilling geometry, and attachment points.

Do not place impossible perfectly sharp interior corners on components supposedly produced with conventional milling tools.

### Turned shafts

Use proper circular geometry, shoulders, grooves, retaining-ring channels, threaded ends where applicable, and axial transitions.

Fine lathe marks must follow the circumferential direction.

### Sheet-metal elements

Use realistic thickness, controlled bends, appropriate bend radii, and attachment flanges.

### Molded polymer parts

Include plausible parting lines, wall-thickness logic, mounting bosses, and internal reinforcement where appropriate.

### Additively manufactured replacement parts

A small number of non-original components may show subtle manufacturing characteristics consistent with metal additive manufacturing followed by machining of critical surfaces.

Avoid exaggerated layer lines.

### Sealed electronic assemblies

Include protective enclosures, cable glands, connector seats, fastening screws, and believable cable exits.

The arm must feel like it was assembled from individually manufactured components, not sculpted from one material.

# PART III — SINGLE-AGENT PRODUCTION SYSTEM

# 11. MANDATORY MODELING METHODOLOGY

Follow a strict sequential production process.

The hierarchy is:

COMPLETE ARM → MAJOR SYSTEMS → SUBASSEMBLIES → INDIVIDUAL COMPONENTS → MECHANICAL FEATURES → MICRODETAILS.

Before detailed modeling:

1. Define the entire mechanical architecture.
2. Establish accurate dimensions.
3. Build a complete low-detail blockout.
4. Determine how assemblies connect.
5. Identify all critical joint axes.
6. Determine necessary internal volumes.
7. Create the component registry.
8. Establish production dependencies.

After the blockout, develop one component or tightly coupled assembly at a time.

**Do not spread unfinished low-quality geometry across the whole model.**

For each component, perform the following loop:

1. Technical planning.
2. Primary geometry.
3. Structural geometry.
4. Secondary geometry.
5. Functional fasteners and interfaces.
6. Manufacturing characteristics.
7. PBR materials.
8. History-driven surface condition.
9. Geometric and shader microdetail.
10. Individual inspection.
11. Integration inspection.
12. Targeted corrections.
13. Save checkpoint.
14. Update component registry.

Do not proceed to the next independent component until the current component has passed its critical quality gates.

Mechanically interdependent components may be developed together when required.

For example, paired joint bearings and their shafts must be dimensioned together.

# 13. MANDATORY QUALITY GATES

Every important assembly must pass seven gates.

**Gate A — Geometry:** Correct dimensions, silhouette, thickness, bevels, normals, topology where relevant, and no unintended intersections.

**Gate B — Construction:** Plausible manufacturing, load transfer, assembly, fastening, support, and clearances.

**Gate C — Detail:** All required secondary geometry, important fasteners, seals, recesses, mechanical stops, and meaningful microfeatures exist.

**Gate D — Materials:** Correct metallic and dielectric response, texture scale, roughness, anisotropy, material transitions, and manufacturing characteristics.

**Gate E — Historical realism:** Wear, repairs, replacements, and protected surfaces reflect the established history.

**Gate F — Photographic credibility:** The part remains physically convincing under neutral lighting and extreme close-ups.

**Gate G — Integration:** The part connects, moves, contacts, and aligns correctly with neighboring systems.

Allowed gate results:

- PASS
- FAIL
- PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS
- NOT_VERIFIED

Any critical failure requires correction before the component can be declared complete.

# 15. EXTREME FASTENER REQUIREMENTS

Fasteners are not decoration.

For every important screw or bolt, determine:

- What components it connects.
- Whether it is accessible during assembly.
- Whether the fastening direction makes sense.
- Whether sufficient material exists around the hole.
- Whether a washer or threaded insert is appropriate.
- Whether the head should be recessed or exposed.
- Whether the fastener is original or replaced.
- Whether service wear is justified.

Use a coherent family of miniature metric fasteners, such as M2, M2.5, M3, and M4 where mechanically appropriate.

Do not distribute every size everywhere.

Model actual recessed socket geometry on important hero-visible screws.

For visible screw threads, use genuine helical geometry when the camera distance makes that detail meaningful.

Hidden fasteners may use simplified geometry where appropriate.

Make sure bolts clamp actual surfaces.

Do not use screws that disappear into empty space.

Standardized fasteners should be intelligently instanced and reused.

Individual instances may have justified variation in orientation, finish, or wear.

# 16. REALISTIC MICROGEOMETRY

Apply microgeometry only where physically appropriate.

### Machined metal

Use fine directional marks based on the machining process.

Approximate visual scales may include:

- Submillimeter edge chamfers.
- Fine tool marks visible under macro lighting.
- Minute variations in surface roughness.
- Tiny drilled-hole chamfers.
- Faint turning patterns on cylindrical components.
- Subtle polishing around mating surfaces.

### Painted or coated metal

Represent the coating as a thin surface finish.

When damaged, it may reveal a different underlying substrate.

Corresponding color, roughness, normal detail, and edge geometry must agree.

Do not create scratches that change color without affecting the surface response.

### Finger contact pads

Model:

- Actual pad thickness.
- Mounting lips.
- Compression-related curvature.
- Surface grain.
- Minor contact polish.
- Fine debris embedded in protected edges.
- Small variations between worn and replaced pads.

### Tool-contact damage

If a screw head has been damaged by a tool, represent the physical deformation or corresponding normal detail.

Localized marks must respect the geometry and direction of contact.

### Protected recesses

Use subtle contamination where appropriate.

Do not place equal amounts of dust on exposed and sealed surfaces.
